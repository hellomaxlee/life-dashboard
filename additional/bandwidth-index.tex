\section*{Status}
Implemented in \texttt{app/metrics/bandwidth.py} and stored per day in
\texttt{daily\_metrics}. It replaces the first draft, which a formal audit and a
signal-validity review took apart (Section~\ref{sec:dropped}). Sleep is scored on
bands Max set himself; sleeping heart rate is compared with his own previous four
weeks. The score has not been checked against how he felt, and no self-report
will be collected, so it is a statement about the night and not a measurement of
mood or judgement.

\section{The Sleep Term}
Max's ruling of 20 November 2026: too much sleep costs what too little does
(``it's not too much about deprivation that is the issue for me, but rather
oversleeping''). The term is his bands, joined by straight lines and flat beyond
either end:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrrrr}
\toprule
Hours & 4.5 & 5.5 & 6.0 & 6.5 & 7.0 & 8.5 & 9.0 & 9.5 & 10.0 & 11.0 \\
$z_s$ & $-3$ & $-2$ & 0 & 0 & $+2$ & $+2$ & 0 & 0 & $-2$ & $-3$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
So $z_s = +2$ from 7 to 8.5 hours, 0 from 6 to 6.5 and from 9 to 9.5, and $-2$
at 5.5 and at 10. It uses no history, so it exists from the first night.

\section{The Heart-Rate Term}
Let $H$ be the sleeping heart rates on days $d-28, \dots, d-1$, censored nights
left out. The term is absent when $x_h$ is absent or $|H| < 14$.
\begin{align}
m &= \med(H) \\
\sigma &= \max\!\big(1.4826 \cdot \med_{v \in H} |v - m|,\; 2.0\ \text{bpm}\big) \\
z_h &= \clamp\!\Big(-\frac{x_h - m}{\sigma},\, -3,\, 3\Big)
\end{align}

\section{Censored Nights and Absent Terms}
A censored night saw only part of the night. Its hours are a lower bound, and the
first half of a night runs above the second, so neither term may go below zero:
\begin{equation}
c \implies z_s \leftarrow \max(z_s, 0), \qquad z_h \leftarrow \max(z_h, 0)
\end{equation}
An absent term has $z_i = 0$. Weights are never renormalised.

\section{The Index and the Cue}
\begin{equation}
B = 50 + \frac{25}{3}\,(z_s + z_h)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{cue on} \iff \text{at least one term is present and } z_s + z_h \le -2
\end{equation}
With both terms absent there is no reading: $B$ is stored as null, never 50, and
the cue is off. The cue is decided on the $z$ sum, not on the rounded $B$, and is
a pure function of the stored data and the clock, recomputed on every engine run.

One carry rule keeps the lamp from changing at midnight: today, before noon, with
no night on record yet, shows yesterday's cue and score. A past day never
carries, and a carried value is not carried again.

$B$ is an affine label for the $z$ sum: $100/3$ is the cue line, 50 a middling
night, $200/3$ a night in the green band with an ordinary heart rate. It is not a
percentage and not a percentile.

\section{Properties}
\begin{claim}[Range]
Whenever $B$ is defined, $0 \le B \le 275/3 \approx 91.67$, with no clamp applied
to $B$. In particular $B$ never leaves $[0, 100]$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$z_s \in [-3, 2]$ because a piecewise-linear function takes its extremes at its
knots, and $z_h \in [-3, 3]$ by its clamp; either may be 0. So
$z_s + z_h \in [-6, 5]$ and $B \in [0,\, 50 + 125/3]$. Both ends are attained.
\end{proof}
\begin{claim}[Shape]
On valid inputs, $B$ is non-increasing in $x_h$. In $x_s$ it is continuous,
non-decreasing up to 7 hours, constant to 8.5, and non-increasing after.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$m$ and $\sigma > 0$ depend only on history; clamping and $\max(\cdot, 0)$
preserve order. The sleep knots rise to 7, hold to 8.5, and fall after.
\end{proof}
At the edge of validity an input becomes absent and its term returns to 0, which
is a jump by design: 16.01 hours is not a night.
\begin{claim}[Bounded Sensitivity]
One bpm moves $B$ by at most $25/6 \approx 4.17$ points, because
$\sigma \ge 2.0$. A tenth of an hour of sleep moves it by at most
$10/3 \approx 3.33$, on the steepest sides (5.5 to 6, 6.5 to 7, 8.5 to 9, 9.5 to
10), and by nothing inside a band.
\end{claim}
\begin{claim}[Missing Data]
An absent term changes $B$ by exactly its own contribution; a censored night
never lowers $B$; missing data alone cannot turn the cue on.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The terms are added with fixed weights. For a censored night both terms are
$\ge 0$, so $z_s + z_h \ge 0 > -2$. With no term present the cue is off unless
carried, and a carried cue rests on yesterday's reading.
\end{proof}

\section{The Fuller Score, a Shadow}
Max, 20 November 2026: robust ``isn't necessarily to decrease the number of
variables \dots\ partially means defensibility and partially means completeness
as a proxy for my actual bandwidth''. A second score with ten terms is stored
beside the first. Nothing displays it; the lamp still shows the two-term score.
\begin{equation}
B_{\text{full}} = 50 + \frac{1}{6} \sum_i w_i z_i, \qquad \sum_i w_i = 100
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{0.20\textwidth}L{0.47\textwidth}rr}
\toprule
Domain & Term & Weight & Range of $z$ \\
\midrule
Sleep Amount & Last night, on his bands & 30 & $[-3, 2]$ \\
 & Mean band score of the three nights before & 14 & $[-3, 2]$ \\
Quality and Timing & Deep-plus-REM share and minutes awake & 5 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
 & Sleep onset against his usual, later is worse & 12 & $[-3, 1]$ \\
Recovery & Sleeping heart rate & 24 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
 & $\ln$ of yesterday's HRV & 4 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
 & Yesterday's resting heart rate & 3 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
Load & Yesterday's acute over chronic, overreach only & 2 & $[-3, 0]$ \\
Movement and Light & Yesterday's steps & 4 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
 & Yesterday's minutes of daylight & 2 & $[-3, 3]$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Terms without a band of their own are robust $z$ scores against the prior 28
days, as in Section 3. An absent term adds 0, and the trace records how much of
the hundred was present.

\begin{claim}[Range]
$0 \le B_{\text{full}} \le 50 + 226/6 \approx 87.67$, with no clamp on the result.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Each $z_i$ lies in its own range above, so $\sum w_i z_i \in [-300, 226]$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The Cue Line Follows from the Weights.} A weighted mean of ten terms
spreads less than a sum of two. If the terms were independent with equal spread,
the spread of $\sum w_i z_i$ would be proportional to $\sqrt{\sum w_i^2}$. The
lamp's score is the same form with weights $(50, 50)$ and its cue at $-100$, so
\begin{equation}
\ell = -100 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\sum w_i^2}}{\sqrt{50^2 + 50^2}} = -100 \cdot
\frac{\sqrt{1890}}{\sqrt{5000}} \approx -61.48
\end{equation}
and the cue is on when $\sum w_i z_i \le \ell$ with at least 60 of the hundred
present: $B_{\text{full}} \le 39.75$. Green starts at the mirror image, 60.25.
Either anchor term reaches the line alone: $30 \cdot (-3) = -90$ and
$24 \cdot (-3) = -72$.

\paragraph{What It Can Claim.} The weights are priors, set from how repeatable
each input is on his data and a stated guess at its relevance. Two reviews found
that the sleep bands still carry about half of all movement in the score, that it
correlates 0.95 with the two-term score, and that under a $\pm 50\%$ perturbation
of the weights 93.7\% of days keep their colour. So the fuller score is broader
and stable, and no more proven than the first. The inputs that would widen it
most are not built: a same-day sedentary run, hours awake, sleeping respiratory
rate and wrist temperature.

\section{The Trace}
Each day stores, per term, $x$, the reference, the scale, $z$, the points
$\tfrac{25}{3} z$, and the history count, plus the censored flag and the number
of heart-rate readings. \texttt{uv run python -m tools.metrics -{}-show DATE}
prints it. Fifty plus the two point values re-adds to $B$ within 0.01.

\section{A Worked Case}
History: fourteen nights at a sleeping heart rate of 55 bpm, so the scale sits at
its floor. Last night: 5.5 h at 57 bpm.
\begin{align*}
z_s &= -2 \quad \text{(the knot at 5.5)} & z_h &= -(57 - 55)/2.0 = -1.0 \\
B &= 50 + \tfrac{25}{3}(-3) = 25.00 & &\text{cue on}
\end{align*}
The same heart rate after 7.5 h gives $z_s = +2$ and $B = 58.33$.

\section{On the Panel}
The number is never shown. It colours the shade of the lamp on the lower Books
shelf, and nothing else refers to it:

\toprule
Score & Shade \\
\midrule
Cue on ($B \le 100/3$) & Red \\
$100/3 < B < 50$ & Red through orange to gold \\

rounded score, decides red. Until today's night arrives, and no later than noon,
the lamp keeps yesterday's colour.

\section{Known Limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item No criterion. Nothing is logged by hand, so the bands and the weights are
  Max's judgement and priors, and the score can be audited for arithmetic, not
  for truth.
\item A night in the green band cannot reach the cue on heart rate alone: the
  lowest it can score is $50 + \tfrac{25}{3}(2 - 3) = 41.67$, orange. A night of
  illness with ordinary hours shows as orange, not red.
\item A night with no sleep record gives no reading, and those are common.
\item Sleeping heart rate is taken over the whole session, awake minutes included.
\item A night that begins before 18:00 is a nap by the dashboard's rules and gives
  no reading.
\item The bands are fixed numbers. If his need changes, they are edited by hand.
\end{itemize}
