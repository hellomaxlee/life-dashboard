\section*{Status}
Implemented in \texttt{app/metrics/bandwidth.py} and stored per day in
\texttt{daily\_metrics}. It replaces the first draft, which a formal audit and a
signal-validity review took apart (Section~\ref{sec:dropped}). The score compares
last night with Max's own previous four weeks. It has not been checked against how
he felt, and no self-report will be collected, so it is a statement about the
night and not a measurement of mood or judgement.

\section{Standardisation}
One rule for both terms. Let $H_i$ be the values of input $i$ on days
$d-28, \dots, d-1$, censored nights left out. A term is absent when its input is
absent or $|H_i| < 14$.
\begin{align}
m_i &= \med(H_i) \\
\sigma_i &= \max\!\big(1.4826 \cdot \med_{v \in H_i} |v - m_i|,\; f_i\big),
  \qquad f_s = 1.0\ \text{h},\quad f_h = 2.0\ \text{bpm}
\end{align}
The sleep reference is capped, because a median above eight hours reflects
catch-up sleep and not need:
\begin{equation}
r_s = \min(m_s,\, 8.0), \qquad r_h = m_h
\end{equation}
With $\tilde z_s = (x_s - r_s)/\sigma_s$ and $\tilde z_h = -(x_h - r_h)/\sigma_h$:
\begin{align}
z_s &= \clamp(\tilde z_s,\, -3,\, 1) \\
z_h &= \clamp(\tilde z_h,\, -3,\, 3)
\end{align}
The sleep term stops at $+1$: a long night is not evidence of a good one, and at
$+3$ a very long night could cancel a raised sleeping heart rate.

A censored night saw only part of the night. Its hours are a lower bound, and the
first half of a night runs above the second, so neither term may go below zero:
\begin{equation}
c \implies z_s \leftarrow \max(z_s, 0), \qquad z_h \leftarrow \max(z_h, 0)
\end{equation}
An absent term has $z_i = 0$. Weights are never renormalised.

\section{The Index and the Cue}
\begin{equation}
B = 50 + \frac{25}{3}\,(z_s + z_h)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{cue on} \iff \text{at least one term is present and } z_s + z_h \le -2
\end{equation}
With both terms absent there is no reading: $B$ is stored as null, never 50, and
the cue is off. The cue is decided on the $z$ sum, not on the rounded $B$, and is
a pure function of the stored data and the clock, recomputed on every engine run.

One carry rule keeps the cue from switching off at midnight: today, before noon,
with no night on record yet, shows yesterday's cue. A past day never carries, and
a carried cue is not carried again.

$B$ is an affine label for the $z$ sum. Fifty is a night like his usual; $100/3$
is the cue line. It is not a percentage and not a percentile.

\section{Properties}
\begin{claim}[Range]
Whenever $B$ is defined, $0 \le B \le 250/3 \approx 83.33$, with no clamp applied
to $B$. In particular $B$ never leaves $[0, 100]$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$z_s \in [-3, 1]$ and $z_h \in [-3, 3]$ by their own clamps, or equal 0, so
$z_s + z_h \in [-6, 4]$ and $B \in [50 - 50,\, 50 + 100/3]$. Both ends are
attained.
\end{proof}
\begin{claim}[Monotonicity]
On valid inputs, $B$ is non-decreasing in $x_s$ and non-increasing in $x_h$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
$r_i$ and $\sigma_i > 0$ depend only on history, so each $\tilde z_i$ is monotone
in its input; clamping and $\max(\cdot, 0)$ preserve order.
\end{proof}
At the edge of validity an input becomes absent and its term returns to 0, which
is a jump by design: 16.01 hours is not a night.
\begin{claim}[Bounded Sensitivity]
One hour of sleep moves $B$ by at most $25/3 \approx 8.33$ points and one bpm by
at most $25/6 \approx 4.17$, because $\sigma_s \ge 1.0$ and $\sigma_h \ge 2.0$.
\end{claim}
\begin{claim}[Missing Data]
An absent term changes $B$ by exactly its own contribution; a censored night
never lowers $B$; missing data alone cannot turn the cue on.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The terms are added with fixed weights. For a censored night both terms are
$\ge 0$, so $z_s + z_h \ge 0 > -2$. With no term present the cue is off unless
carried, and a carried cue rests on yesterday's reading.
\end{proof}

\section{The Trace}
Each day stores, per term, $x$, the reference, the scale, $z$, the points
$\tfrac{25}{3} z$, and the history count, plus the censored flag and the number
of heart-rate readings. \texttt{uv run python -m tools.metrics -{}-show DATE}
prints it. Fifty plus the two point values re-adds to $B$ within 0.01.

\section{A Worked Case}
History: fourteen nights of 7.5 h at a sleeping heart rate of 55 bpm, so both
scales sit at their floors. Last night: 6.0 h at 57 bpm.
\begin{align*}
z_s &= (6.0 - 7.5)/1.0 = -1.5 & z_h &= -(57 - 55)/2.0 = -1.0 \\
B &= 50 + \tfrac{25}{3}(-2.5) = 29.17 & &\text{cue on}
\end{align*}

\section{On the Panel}
The number is never shown. While the cue is on, the lamp on the lower Books shelf
is unlit; otherwise it is lit. No word, colour change, or mark elsewhere refers
to it.

\section{On Max's Data}

\section{Known Limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item No criterion. Nothing is logged by hand, so the weights stay priors and the
  score can be audited for arithmetic, not for truth.
\item A night with no sleep record gives no reading, and those are common.
\item Sleeping heart rate is taken over the whole session, awake minutes included.
\item A night that begins before 18:00 is a nap by the dashboard's rules and gives
  no reading.
\item Sleeping heart rate alone turns the cue on at 4 bpm over his median. The
  mathematician would raise that floor to 3 bpm; the biostatistician would not.
\item Marking a past night censored changes the baseline and can move a later day
  across the line.
\end{itemize}
